# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
People who already know a lot of English and want to use more of it, in two groups, in this order:

1. Non-native professionals (starting with Spanish speakers in Latin America) who work in English every day and want to sound precise instead of approximate.
2. Native speakers who read widely and want rarer, more exact words.

They meet this site from the App Store listing (support and privacy links), from a shared link, or from a search for the app by name. They are on a phone as often as a laptop. They are readers: they will read a paragraph if it is worth reading.

## Product Purpose
headwordapp.com is the public face of Headword, an iPhone app: one new English word a day, delivered as a full dictionary entry on the Lock Screen, and a 60-second drill that has you write with the word before you can say you have learned it. The wedge against Vocabulary, WordUp and word-of-the-day apps is production, not recognition.

The site has three pages: the landing page, Support and the Privacy policy. The landing page has one job: make a reader want the app and give them one thing to do about it (before launch, ask to be told when it ships; after launch, the App Store link). Success is a reader who understands in ten seconds what makes Headword different and taps the one action.

A hard constraint carried by the privacy policy: nothing on the site loads from a third party. No web fonts, no scripts, no analytics, no embeds. Everything is served from this repository by GitHub Pages.

## Brand Personality
Typeset, quiet, exacting. The voice of a good dictionary entry: precise, unhurried, a little dry, never salesy. It says exactly what is free and exactly what is paid. It names its sources. It uses the app's own material (entries, respellings, apparatus labels, real screens) rather than illustrations or metaphors.

The interface speaks English and Spanish; the words are English.

## Anti-references
- Generic App Store template sites: gradient hero, floating phone mockups, feature icon grids, star ratings.
- Gamified language apps: mascots, bright greens, confetti, streak theatre.
- Dark startup landing pages: near-black background, one neon accent, monospace labels.
- Magazine affectation: drop caps, italic display serifs, broadsheet columns beyond what a dictionary entry needs.

## Design Principles
- Show the entry, not an illustration. The product's own material is the imagery.
- The site is typeset like the app: the same serif, the same paper, the same red, the same apparatus labels (In use, Origin).
- One action per page, and it is honest about the state of the app.
- Every claim is specific: what is free, what is paid, where the words come from, where the data lives.
- Nothing from anyone else. The privacy page's promise is a design constraint.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text and quiet text both clear 4.5:1 on paper in light and dark. Visible keyboard focus. Motion is one page-load reveal and it is removed under prefers-reduced-motion. Screenshots carry real alt text. The page works at 320px wide with no horizontal scroll and at 200% zoom.
