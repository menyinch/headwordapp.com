The best apps to improve your English vocabulary for work

Updated 28 October 2026 · By Menyin Chang, who makes Headword. Headword is one of the apps below; the others are described as their own App Store pages describe them.

Short answer. If you already work in English and want to use better words, not only recognize them, choose an app that makes you produce the word: type it, say it, write a sentence with it. Headword does that for iPhone. WordUp is the broadest word list, Elevate trains general communication, and Anki is best for your own work terms.

How to choose

Most vocabulary apps test recognition: you see a word and pick its meaning from four options. That is how a word becomes familiar. It is not how a word becomes yours. A word you can use in a meeting is one you have had to produce, from memory, in a sentence of your own. The research behind this is old and consistent: words you generate or say aloud are remembered better than words you only read (Slamecka & Graf, 1978; MacLeod et al., 2010), and being tested beats rereading (Karpicke & Roediger, 2008).

Four questions sort the apps quickly:

  • Does it make you produce the word, or only pick it from a list?
  • Are the words useful at work, or rare words chosen to impress?
  • Does it tell you the register: formal, neutral, informal? A word that fits an essay can sound odd in an email.
  • Does it bring words back before you forget them (spaced repetition)?

Headword

Choose it if you already work in English, your sentences are correct but plain, and you want to sound precise instead of approximate. Each day brings one word as a full dictionary entry: definition, register note (formal, literary, informal), two example sentences, a General American IPA transcription and a recorded pronunciation, synonyms, opposites and the words it usually goes with. Practice then makes you use it: complete a sentence by typing the word, spell it from its sound, choose the word that fits the tone, say it aloud, and write your own sentence. As a word gets stronger the questions get harder. Review brings words back on a spaced-repetition schedule (FSRS).

Skip it if you use Android or an iPad-first setup, you are a beginner (the words assume upper-intermediate English), or you want hundreds of new words a week. Headword is deliberately slow: one word a day free, five or ten with Plus. About 4,000 words in total.

WordUp

Choose it if you want breadth. WordUp maps what you already know against a 25,000-word list ranked by usefulness, then teaches the gaps with varied challenges, spaced repetition and an AI chat tutor. Skip it if you want depth on fewer words, or you mostly need help choosing the right word for a formal email.

WordUp on the App Store

Elevate

Choose it if vocabulary is one part of a wider goal: focus, memory, reading speed and speaking confidence, trained in short daily games. Skip it if you specifically want English vocabulary for work: Elevate is a general brain-training app, written for native speakers.

Elevate on the App Store

AnkiMobile

Choose it if your problem is your own field's terms: compliance, medicine, engineering, a client's product names. You write the cards, and Anki's scheduler is among the best. Skip it if you want someone else to choose the words, write the examples and tell you the register: Anki gives you an empty box.

AnkiMobile on the App Store

Vocabulary.com

Choose it if you enjoy quiz-style learning and want an app that adapts to your level, with a strong web version. Skip it if you want to practice producing words rather than recognizing them.

Vocabulary.com on the App Store

Vocabulary (Monkey Taps)

Choose it if you want a light habit: a word on your Lock Screen every day, with very little to do. Skip it if you want the word to stay: seeing a word is exposure, not practice.

Vocabulary on the App Store

Where Headword falls short

It is iPhone only. It does not grade your free-written sentences: you compare yours with the entry's examples and rate it yourself. The interface is in English and Spanish only, though the words are English for everyone. And at one word a day, the free version is a habit, not a course.
